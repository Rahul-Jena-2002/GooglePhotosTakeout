Guides & Specifications

Learn how to export Google Photos, restore missing EXIF metadata, and import your library back with the correct dates and locations—all in a few simple steps.

01

Navigate to Google Takeout takeout.google.com

Open Google Takeout in a new browser tab. Make sure you are logged into the Google Account that contains the Google Photos library you wish to export.

Go to Google Takeout
02

Select Google Photos Only

By default, Google selects all of your Google Account data. Click "Deselect all" at the top of the list, then scroll down to find Google Photos and check its box. This makes your download size significantly smaller and saves you time.

03

Create Export and Download ZIPs

Scroll to the bottom, click "Next step", select your preferred archive sizes (we recommend 10GB or 50GB splits if you have a massive library to avoid downloading dozens of small files), and click "Create export". Google will generate your files and email you download links. Download all of these ZIP files onto your computer.

04

Load Archive in TakeoutFix

Go to the Restore Center. Click "Select Folder" to select your extracted Google Takeout folder directly on your hard drive, or drop your downloaded ZIP archives directly into the drop zone. TakeoutFix automatically scans your Google Takeout folder and detects photos, videos, and their matching metadata files.

05

Rebuild EXIF Metadata Offline

Click "Start Restore." TakeoutFix automatically matches each photo and video with its corresponding metadata file and restores missing dates, GPS locations, and EXIF information. Everything happens locally in your browser, so your files never leave your device. Once completed, save your restored files.

06

Transfer to Your Phone or Tablet

Once you have downloaded the restored files to your computer, we highly recommend transferring them back to your device in a ZIP archive. Standard USB transfers strip file creation dates, while unzipping directly on the device preserves them perfectly:

Apple iPhone / iPad (iOS)

  • AirDrop (Mac): Send the restored `.zip` archive directly to your iPhone or iPad, and select the native Files app to extract it. This preserves all timestamps.
  • iCloud Photos: Upload files via iCloud.com on your computer to sync them automatically.
  • Avoid Finder USB Sync: Do not sync raw folders via USB Sync, as iOS resets filesystem dates during folder synchronization.

Android Devices

  • Unzip on Device: Copy the restored `.zip` archive to your device via USB or Quick Share, then extract it directly using the Files by Google app to keep file system timestamps intact.
  • Avoid Raw USB Transfer: Do not copy raw folders via USB MTP mode directly to your Android gallery, as Android will overwrite file creation/modification dates with the transfer time.
  • Google Photos Sync: Drag and drop files to photos.google.com in your computer's browser to sync them to your phone.

⏱️ Typical Restore Times

TakeoutFix scans your archive in a few seconds and restores metadata locally. Most libraries finish in under a few minutes, while very large Google Takeout archives may take longer depending on your computer and archive size.

Phase 01

Local Directory Parsing Heuristics

Secure Client-Side Indexing

TakeoutFix utilizes the modern browser File System Access API to gain read-only permission to your Google Takeout folder. It executes a high-performance recursive traversal to locate all media files and their corresponding sidecar JSON files entirely on your machine.

  • Zero-upload architecture: media bytes never leave your device.
  • Asynchronous scanning runs in a background Web Worker to prevent UI blocking.
  • Supports nested folder structures and multi-part Takeout archives.

Phase 02

Heuristic Metadata Matching Algorithms

Pairing Files with Sidecars

Google Takeout often edits names, truncates titles, or appends suffixes (like '-edited' or '(1)') to media, making standard matching fail. TakeoutFix applies a multi-layered match heuristic to pair files with their JSON sidecar, resolving filename inconsistencies automatically.

  • Name-hash pairing checks original file titles inside the JSON schema.
  • Fuzzy matching reconciles truncated strings and system-appended suffixes.
  • Timestamp alignment acts as a fallback to resolve similar media files.

Phase 03

Deep EXIF Header Injection Mechanics

Rebuilding Media Headers

Once matched, the engine extracts the Unix epoch timestamps and coordinates. It parses the binary structures JPEGs, PNGs, and HEICs, injecting parameters directly into EXIF headers (DateTimeOriginal and GPS tags) without transcoding files.

  • Reconstructs native binary EXIF tags without re-compressing the image.
  • Updates QuickTime/UserData metadata headers inside MP4/MOV videos.
  • Outputs clean, linkable files ready for Apple Photos or Google Photos import.
